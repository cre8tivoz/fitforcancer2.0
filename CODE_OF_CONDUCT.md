# Code of Conduct

Fit For Cancer is a free, open-source project built to support people in Australia going through cancer treatment, as well as the carers, friends and family members supporting them.

That makes the tone of this project important.

You do not need to be a developer, designer, clinician, cancer expert, carer or someone with lived experience to contribute. You just need to be thoughtful, respectful and willing to work with other people in good faith.

Fit For Cancer is currently maintained by one developer, so clear communication and coordination matter just as much as good ideas.

## Be a decent person

When taking part in this project:

- be respectful, even when you disagree;
- assume people are trying to help unless there is a clear reason not to;
- explain feedback clearly and constructively;
- criticise the work, not the person;
- make room for people with different levels of technical knowledge and lived experience;
- remember that contributors and users may be dealing with cancer, fatigue, caregiving or other difficult circumstances;
- keep discussions focused on improving the project.

There is no expectation that contributors need to share personal health information or explain why they care about the project.

## Coordinate before making big changes

Fit For Cancer is a small project with a solo maintainer.

Please open an issue or start a discussion before investing significant time in a large redesign, architecture change, new AI framework, major dependency, clinical-content change or new product direction.

This is not about controlling contributions. It is about avoiding duplicated work, conflicting changes and people spending hours on something that does not fit the current direction.

Small bug fixes, accessibility improvements, documentation corrections and focused reliability improvements can usually go straight to a pull request.

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) for the areas where help is most useful right now.

## Health information needs extra care

Fit For Cancer is a supportive tool. It is not a replacement for a doctor, oncology team, dietitian, physiotherapist or other qualified health professional.

Contributors are welcome to question, improve and review health-related content, but please do not:

- present personal experience as universal medical advice;
- make unsupported claims about cancer treatment, cures, supplements or alternative therapies;
- encourage people to ignore or replace evidence-based treatment;
- introduce clinical guidance without appropriate evidence and maintainer review.

You do not need medical qualifications to contribute to the project. Clinical and safety-sensitive changes simply receive a higher level of review.

## What is not acceptable

The following behaviour is not welcome in project spaces:

- harassment, threats or intimidation;
- discrimination or demeaning comments;
- personal attacks;
- sexual harassment or unwanted sexual attention;
- deliberately inflammatory or hostile behaviour;
- repeated bad-faith arguments after a decision has been explained;
- publishing someone else's private information;
- spam, scams or promotional contributions unrelated to the project;
- knowingly introducing misleading or unsafe health information.

Project spaces include issues, pull requests, discussions, code review, documentation, email conversations about the project and other official Fit For Cancer contributor channels.

## Disagreement is fine

People will disagree about design, code, AI behaviour, accessibility, health information and product direction. That is normal.

Useful disagreement explains the problem, gives enough context for other people to understand it and stays open to a different solution.

A contribution may be declined even when it is technically good. That does not mean the contributor has done anything wrong. The project has limited maintenance capacity and sometimes needs to choose one direction over another.

## Maintainer responsibilities

The maintainer may edit or remove comments, close issues or pull requests, reject contributions, or restrict participation when behaviour is clearly harmful to the project or its community.

Where practical, problems will be handled first with a clear explanation and an opportunity to correct the behaviour. Serious harassment, threats, privacy violations, spam or deliberate attempts to cause harm may result in immediate removal or blocking.

## Reporting a problem

If something happens in the project that you do not feel comfortable raising publicly, email:

**Jaquesdesign@proton.me**

Please include enough information to understand what happened, such as links to the relevant issue, pull request or comment where possible.

Reports will be handled as privately as reasonably possible. Because Fit For Cancer is currently maintained by one person, responses may not always be immediate, but genuine conduct or safety concerns will be taken seriously.

## The basic rule

Help make the project better, and treat the people around it like people.

That is enough.
